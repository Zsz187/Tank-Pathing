                         Tank Pathing Guide
                    

                    
                

                

            

            

                

                    
Quick start

                    
  1. Enter your robot’s size and wheel details in the Robot tab (left panel).
  2. Pick a preset in the top bar, or press Clear in the Waypoints tab and click Add Waypoint to build your own path.
  3. Drag the numbered circles on the field to place waypoints. Drag the tip of each arrow to set the robot’s heading.
  4. Add waits and actions (attachment motors, mission interactions) to any waypoint.
  5. Press Play under the field to watch the run, then press Export to get code for your robot.
Waypoint 1 is the start. The robot turns to face the next waypoint, drives straight to it, then turns to that waypoint’s heading — the standard FLL step-by-step tank-drive method.

                

                

                    
Top bar

                    
  • Presets: loads an example path (Basic L-Drive, 3-Mission Loop, SW to SE Arc). This replaces your current waypoints.
  • Snap WP1: SW Launch / SE Launch put the robot in that bottom corner, touching both walls, facing 0° (east) or 180° (west).
  • Wall menu: keeps the first waypoint flush against a wall. Any (nearest) is the default: it snaps to whichever of the four walls is closest, and into the corner when a second wall is within 30 cm. The Gap box sets the space between robot and wall in mm (0 = touching; use a small negative number to push it closer). You can also lock it to Bottom, Top, Left, Right, SW corner or SE corner using your robot’s length, width and heading. It follows you when you drag, turn or resize the robot. Choose Off for free placement.
  • cm / mm / in: changes the units shown in inputs and on the field. Exported code always uses the units of its platform (see Export).
  • ?: opens this guide.  Export: opens the code window.

                

                

                    
Waypoints tab

                    
  • Add Waypoint adds a point 30 cm after the last one. Clear removes all points.
  • Each card has X, Y and Heading boxes. Type exact numbers, or just drag on the field. Heading 0° faces right (east), 90° faces up (north).
  • Use the ↑ ↓ buttons to reorder points and the bin to delete one.
  • On the field, drag a circle to move it, or drag the small dot at the end of its arrow to rotate it. Touch screens work too.
  • A red or green badge in the Field tab tells you whether the start position fits inside the launch area.
  • Optimize path: removes waypoints that sit on a straight run, and near-duplicates, so the path has fewer steps. It never changes the headings of the waypoints that remain, never removes the first or last waypoint, and never removes one that has actions. The tolerance menu sets how far from the straight line a point may be. Press Undo to restore the old path.

                

                

                    
Waits & actions

                    
Every waypoint card has Actions on arrival at the bottom. Open the + Add… menu to choose:
  • Wait: pause for a number of seconds (for example, to let a mechanism settle).
  • Attachment motor: run a motor on port A–F by a number of degrees at a speed in °/s. Use a port your drive motors don’t use (default D).
  • Interact with mission: pick one of the 15 missions and how many seconds it takes. A dashed line links the waypoint to that mission on the field. In the code it becomes a comment where you paste your attachment routine, plus a wait.
  • Beep: plays a short sound (0.2 s).
Actions run in the order listed, after the robot arrives and finishes its final turn. On waypoint 1 they run before the robot moves. Use ↑ ↓ ✕ on each action to reorder or delete. Their time is added to the run time and the scrubber, and an amber badge on the field shows the count and seconds for that waypoint.

                

                

                    
Robot tab

                    
  • Axle track width: distance between the centres of the two drive wheels. Chassis length / width: the body size used to draw the robot and snap it to walls.
  • Wheel diameter, drive speed / acceleration and turn speed / acceleration set how long moves take and are written into the exported code.
  • Measure your real robot and test; the simulation is only as accurate as these numbers.

                

                

                    
Tuning tab (calibrate your real robot)

                    
Plastic wheels squish a little and axle spacing differs by a few millimetres, so a real robot rarely matches the screen exactly. The Tuning tab fixes that: run a test path, measure what the robot really did, and let the app correct itself.

                    
A. Distance (wheel diameter)

                    
  1. Click 50 cm Straight under Calibration Test Paths.
  2. Export, run it on your mat, and measure how far the robot actually went (say 48.5 cm).
  3. Under Distance Calibration, enter 50 as commanded and 48.5 as measured, then click Adjust Wheel Diameter.

                    
B. Turns (track width, autotune)

                    
  1. Click 180° Spin Test, then export and run it with the robot lined up on a grid line.
  2. Note how far it really turned (say 370°).
  3. Under Turn Calibration (Autotune), enter 180 as target and your measured angle (for example, 190), then click Adjust Track Width.

                    
Also here: SPIKE Prime presets fill in wheel, track and chassis size for common builds, and the 50 cm Square test checks both corrections together. Test paths replace your current waypoints, so export your work first.

                

                

                    
Field tab

                    
  • Launch zone mode: choose the dual arcs, the classic single SW arc, arcs with side storage margins, or none for a custom mat.
  • Field origin: where (0,0) is — SW corner (FLL standard), top-left, or mat centre. Coordinates in the cards follow this choice.
  • Grid, annotations, footprint ghosts: tick boxes to show a 10 cm grid, distance/heading labels, and a faint robot outline at every waypoint.
  • Custom mat image: upload an image, or drag & drop or paste one onto the field. Crop it to the mat edges first so it lines up with the grid. Use the Opacity slider to fade it. The image is remembered in your browser; Clear removes it.
  • Fit Canvas (above the field) re-fits the field to the window.

                

                

                    
Missions tab (BIOGLOW™ 2026–27)

                    
  • Shows all 15 missions as numbered markers (squares are the swappable dock missions 13–15).
  • Score: open a mission card, tick what the robot completes (or enter counts for “each” items) and the total updates. Add the equipment inspection and precision tokens too. Reset score clears it. Ticked missions get a gold ✓ on the field.
  • Click a marker on the field to jump to its card. Locate highlights it; Waypoint here adds a waypoint just below it.
  • Move markers: marker positions are approximate. Turn this on, drag each marker onto its model on your mat, and the spot is remembered. Reset positions restores the defaults.
  • Add BIOGLOW mat image: loads your picture of the mat (see Field tab). Always check the latest official Challenge Updates for rule or point changes.

                

                

                    
Simulation

                    
  • Play / Pause animates the robot along the path, including waits. Reset returns to the start.
  • Drag the timeline slider to scrub to any moment. The Live Telemetry box shows X, Y, heading and distance.
  • Choose 0.5x, 1x or 2x playback speed. The total time (match limit is 2:30) appears beside the slider.

                

                

                    
Export

                    
  • Open Export and pick a tab: Pybricks Python, Pybricks Blocks (steps to copy into the block editor), SPIKE Prime, EV3, or Human Steps (a plain-English guide for the driver).
  • Press Copy to copy the code. Every turn, drive, wait, motor action and mission interaction appears in order.
  • Attachment motors are declared at the top of the code. Check that port letters match your robot.
  • TODO lines mark mission interactions — paste your own attachment code there.
  • Test and calibrate on a real robot: wheel slip, battery level and floor friction change real results.

                

                

                    
Keyboard shortcuts

                    
Space Play / pause R Reset run F Fit field E Open export ? Open guide Esc Close windows Ctrl/Cmd+V Paste mat image
Shortcuts are ignored while you type in a box.

                

                

                    
Tips & troubleshooting

                    
  • First waypoint won’t move off the wall? Set the Wall menu to Off, or slide it along the wall.
  • Start shows not compliant? The whole robot must sit inside a launch area. Use SW / SE Launch, or make the robot smaller in the Robot tab.
  • Missions in the wrong place? Use Move markers and drag them onto your mat image.
  • Mat image too large to remember? It still works this session; re-add it next visit.
  • Your waypoints and settings are not saved when you close the page — export or copy the code before leaving. Only marker positions and the mat image are remembered.
